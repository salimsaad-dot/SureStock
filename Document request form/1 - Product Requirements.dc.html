Product Requirements Document

A modern inventory, sales, and revenue management system for a single retail shop, built to grow into multiple locations.

1. Overview

The shop currently tracks stock, sales, and money in a mix of notebooks, spreadsheets, and memory. That works until it doesn't: stock-outs go unnoticed until a customer asks, slow-moving capital sits on the shelf, and the true margin on any given day is a guess made at closing time.

This system replaces that with one source of truth. Every item that enters the shop is recorded, every sale decrements stock in real time, and every cedi of revenue and cost is attributable to a product, a day, and a staff member. The product is a web application usable on a laptop at the counter and on a phone in the stockroom, and it must keep selling when the internet drops.

Primary outcome: at any moment, the owner can answer three questions in under ten seconds — what do I have, what did I sell, and what did I make.

1.1 Goals

  • Accurate, live stock counts for every product, with a clear audit trail for every change.
  • A fast point-of-sale flow — a typical sale completed in under 20 seconds.
  • Revenue, cost of goods, and gross profit reported daily without manual reconciliation.
  • Automatic low-stock alerts and reorder suggestions based on real sales velocity.
  • Full offline capability at the counter, syncing when the connection returns.

1.2 Non-goals for version 1

  • Full accounting (ledgers, payroll, tax filing). The system exports to accounting, it does not replace it.
  • A customer-facing online store. The data model anticipates it; the build does not include it.
  • Manufacturing or bill-of-materials assembly.
  • Loyalty programmes and marketing automation.

2. Users and roles

CashierCounter staff, possibly part-time.Sell, look up stock, open and close their own till shift. Cannot edit prices, delete sales, or see reports.

Permissions are enforced on the server, not only hidden in the interface. A refund, a price override, or a manual stock adjustment always records who did it and when.

3. Core features

3.1 Product catalogue

Every sellable thing in the shop is a product record: name, SKU, barcode, category, supplier, unit of measure, cost price, selling price, tax treatment, reorder point, and an optional photo. Products may have variants (size, colour, pack size), each with its own SKU and its own stock count. Products can be marked as active, discontinued, or seasonal.

  • Bulk import from a spreadsheet for the initial catalogue load, with a preview-and-fix step before committing.
  • Barcode lookup, plus generated internal barcodes for unlabelled goods.
  • Price history retained — changing a price never rewrites what past sales recorded.

3.2 Inventory tracking

Stock is never edited directly. It is the running total of an append-only log of stock movements, each with a reason: goods received, sale, return, transfer, damage, theft, expiry, or stock-take correction. This makes every discrepancy explainable after the fact.

  • Receiving: record a delivery against a supplier and an optional purchase order; enter quantities and unit costs; the system updates the moving-average cost.
  • Stock takes: a guided count by category or full shop; the system shows expected vs counted and posts the variance as an adjustment with a note.
  • Low-stock alerts: triggered by reorder point, with a suggested order quantity derived from average daily sales and the supplier's lead time.
  • Expiry tracking for perishable goods, with a batch and expiry date on receipt and a "expiring within 30 days" view.

3.3 Sales and point of sale

The selling screen is the most-used surface in the product and is designed for speed: scan or search, quantity, tender, done. It supports mixed payment (part cash, part mobile money), line-level and cart-level discounts with a reason, held carts for customers who step away, and printed or SMS receipts.

  • Payment methods: cash, mobile money, card, and "on account" for known credit customers.
  • Returns and refunds against an original receipt, restoring stock unless marked damaged.
  • Till shifts: opening float, expected cash at close, counted cash, and the variance recorded per staff member.
  • Offline mode: sales queue locally and sync automatically; the cashier sees a clear sync indicator, never a blocked sale.

3.4 Revenue and reporting

Reports answer business questions, not database questions. Each report is filterable by date range, category, staff member, and payment method, and each is exportable to CSV or PDF.

Report Answers
Daily summaryRevenue, transaction count, average basket, cost of goods sold, gross profit, cash vs mobile money split.
Product performanceBest and worst sellers by units and by profit; margin per product; items with no sale in 60 days.
Stock valuationCapital tied up in stock, by category, at cost and at retail.
ShrinkageLosses to damage, expiry, and unexplained variance, by period and by staff shift.
Staff activitySales per cashier, discounts given, refunds processed, till variances.

3.5 Suppliers and purchasing

Supplier records hold contact details, lead time, and payment terms. Purchase orders can be raised from the low-stock list in one action, sent as PDF or WhatsApp message, and later received against — partially or in full. Outstanding supplier balances are visible but not double-entry accounted.

3.6 Settings and administration

Shop profile and receipt header, currency and tax rates, categories, staff accounts and roles, printer and scanner setup, backup and data export, and an activity log covering every sensitive action.

5. Success metrics

  • Median time to complete a two-item sale under 20 seconds after one week of use.
  • Stock accuracy above 98% at the monthly stock take, measured as counted vs expected units.
  • Zero sales lost to system unavailability, including during internet outages.
  • Daily close completed in under five minutes with no manual arithmetic.
  • Stock-outs on the top 50 products reduced by half within three months.

6. Assumptions and open questions

These were assumed while writing and should be confirmed before the build starts.

  • One physical location at launch; the schema supports more from day one.
  • Prices are in Ghana cedis, tax-inclusive, with a configurable rate.
  • Mobile money is a significant share of payments and needs first-class treatment at the till.
  • Roughly 500–3,000 SKUs and up to 300 transactions a day at launch.
  • Hardware: one laptop or tablet at the counter, a USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner, a thermal receipt printer, and staff phones.

Open: Do you need supplier invoice payment tracking in v1? Should returns be allowed without a receipt? Is a customer credit ledger needed at launch, or later?
