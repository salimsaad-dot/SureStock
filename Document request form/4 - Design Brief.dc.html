Design Brief

Colour, type, components, and per-screen guidance for a consistent, calm interface.

1. Design principles

  • Speed over decoration. The sale screen is used hundreds of times a day. Nothing on it exists that does not shorten a transaction.
  • One obvious next action. Every screen has exactly one primary button. Everything else is secondary or quiet.
  • Numbers are the interface. Money and quantities get the largest, clearest type on any screen they appear on, always tabular-aligned.
  • Colour carries meaning, never mood. Green, amber, and red mean exactly one thing each and are never used decoratively.
  • Built for a real counter. Bright shop lighting, a phone held in one hand, a thumb that is not clean. High contrast, large targets, forgiving spacing.

2. Colour palette

A near-neutral base with a single cool ink accent, plus three status colours. Surfaces are warm-neutral rather than pure white so long shifts are easier on the eyes.

Usage rules. Accent is reserved for primary buttons, active navigation, and focus rings — nothing else. Success confirms a completed sale or a synced state; warning marks low stock, offline mode, and pending sync; danger marks refunds, deletions, and negative variances. Never use colour as the only signal: pair it with an icon or a word. All text meets WCAG AA against its surface (4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for large numerals).

3. Typography

One family for the interface, one for numbers. IBM Plex Sans for all UI text: it is legible at small sizes, has a genuine set of weights, and its numerals are unfussy. IBM Plex Mono for money, quantities, SKUs, and receipt numbers, so columns of figures align and misreadings drop.

Never below 13px anywhere in the product; never below 15px on the sell screen. Currency is always written with the symbol and two decimals (GH₵ 24.50), right-aligned in any column of figures.

4. Layout and spacing

  • 4px base unit; spacing steps of 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48. Nothing in between.
  • Corner radius: 6px for inputs, buttons, and cards; 10px for modals and sheets; full for status pills.
  • Elevation: one hairline border for cards, one soft shadow for overlays. No layered or coloured shadows.
  • Touch targets never smaller than 44×44px; cart lines and numeric keypad keys are 56px tall.
  • Breakpoints: phone below 640px (bottom nav, cart as a sheet), tablet 640–1024px, counter/desktop above 1024px (sidebar plus persistent cart pane).

6. Screen-by-screen visual guidance

Sell

Three zones: search and scan bar across the top (56px tall, always focused), a product tile grid filling the middle, and the cart pinned right at roughly one third width. The total sits at the bottom of the cart in display mono, with a full-width accent Charge button beneath it. No sidebar illustration, no promotional space, nothing that scrolls unexpectedly.

Payment and receipt

A modal over a dimmed sell screen. Amount due in display type at the top, method tabs, a large numeric keypad, live change due. On success the modal becomes a confirmation with change due as the largest element on screen and three equal buttons: Print, SMS, New Sale.

Dashboard

Four stat cards in a row, each with a label, a display-size figure, and a small comparison line. Below, a wide revenue chart in a single accent tone on a plain grid, then a two-column split of the attention list and today's top sellers. Generous white space; no more than one chart above the fold.

Product list

A dense table: photo thumbnail, name and SKU stacked, category, stock with a status pill, price, margin. Filter chips above the table rather than a filter drawer. Row height 52px so a full screen shows around fifteen products.

Product detail

A two-column split: the photo and key figures on the left, the movement history as a timeline on the right. Stock on hand is the largest element. Actions sit in a top-right button group.

Stock take

Deliberately stark, designed for a phone: one item per screen, name large, a single big numeric field, and a Next button under the thumb. A progress bar shows items counted. The review step is a plain list of variances, each red or green, with a reason field inline.

Reports

A date-range control and filter chips at the top, then one chart and one table per report. Charts use a single accent tone with a muted comparison series — no multi-colour palettes. Every report ends with an export row.

7. Voice and motion

Copy is plain and specific. "3 items below reorder point", not "Attention required". Errors say what happened and what to do: "Sale not synced yet — it will send when you're back online." No exclamation marks, no jargon, no apologies.

Motion is functional only: 150ms for state changes, 200ms for sheets and modals, ease-out. Adding to the cart animates the line in so the eye can follow it. Nothing else moves, and all motion respects the reduced-motion setting.

8. Deliverables

  • Design tokens as CSS custom properties and a matching Tailwind theme extension.
  • A component library covering the table above, documented in Storybook.
  • High-fidelity mock-ups for Sell, Payment, Dashboard, Product detail, and Stock take at phone and counter widths.
  • An 80mm receipt layout, printed and physically checked before launch.
